# Every age, laid out

Each age group lists, in order (minor everyday details follow the main lists):

- hormonal and body shifts
- the pros and cons of that age
- the big joys and the little joys
- struggles
- sorrows and griefs

Everything listed is **not yet in the simulation**. The "Already in" line at the end of each age shows what is
there now, so nothing is listed twice. Figures are approximate and come from the research named in brackets.

**Built since this list was written** (these items are now simulated, even where a section below still lists them):
- **Illness by age (`illness.py`):** colic-era infections, asthma, eczema, allergies, migraine, insomnia, sleep apnoea,
  blood pressure, diabetes, cancer, stroke, arthritis, osteoporosis, cataracts, prostate and more, with their struggles.
- **Hormones and body (`hormones.py`):**
  - mini-puberty and adrenarche
  - puberty timing, the growth spurt, a first period or a breaking voice
  - the teen body clock, and dopamine gain by age
  - bone and muscle over the years
  - the menstrual cycle, PMS and PMDD
  - pregnancy hormones and the baby blues
  - perimenopause, menopause and HRT
  - low testosterone and couvade
  - the dopamine of new love
  - seasonal depression
  - sleep in old age
- **Hardships (`hardships.py`):**
  - smoking, vaping, cannabis, gambling and opioids
  - car crashes
  - assault and sexual assault
  - offending and prison
  - debt, war and a pandemic
  - suicide loss
  - faith and awe
- **More moments (`seasons.py`):**
  - colic, potty training, jealousy of a new baby, glasses and braces, dyslexia, climate fear
  - prom, homesickness, being asked when he's having kids, the childcare bill, school plays
  - redundancy, affairs, divorce late in life, losing both parents, becoming the oldest generation
  - pills, elderspeak, care homes, financial abuse of the old, great-grandchildren
  - many small joys and annoyances

## 0–1: newborn and infant

**Hormones and body**
- **Mini-puberty (1–6 months).** A surge of testosterone in boys and estrogen in girls. It shapes later
  sex-typed play a little.
- **Cortisol rhythm.** The day-night cortisol rhythm is not there at birth; it settles by about 3 months.
- **Melatonin.** He makes almost none of his own for the first months, which is why newborns sleep in scattered
  bursts. Sleep consolidates at 3–6 months.
- **Growth.** He triples his birth weight in the first year, and growth comes in spurts that line up with fussy
  weeks.
- **Oxytocin.** Feeding and skin-to-skin contact raise oxytocin in both baby and parent.

**Pros:** total plasticity (the brain makes about a million new connections a second), being loved
without condition, and no expectations.
**Cons:** total dependence. He cannot say what is wrong, and the stress of the people caring for him gets under his
skin.

**Joys:** milk when hungry, being rocked, a familiar face, peekaboo (learning that things still exist when hidden),
discovering his own hands and feet.

**Little joys:** warm baths, a soft blanket, a parent's heartbeat, a ceiling fan, the dog.

**Struggles**
- Colic: about 1 in 5 babies, with evening crying that peaks at about 6 weeks.
- Reflux, and growth-spurt fussiness.
- Sleep regressions around 4 months and 8–10 months.
- Starting nursery, which brings a cortisol rise in the first weeks.
- Stranger anxiety from about 8 months.

**Sorrows**
- A carer with postnatal depression gives him less face time.
- Neglect, and hospital stays.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Newborn reflexes: rooting, sucking, the startle (Moro) reflex, and the grasp that can hold an adult's finger.
  Most fade by 4–6 months.
- He sees about 20–30 cm clearly at birth, which is the distance to a feeding parent's face. Colour vision comes in
  over the first months.
- He knows his mother's voice and smell from the womb, and prefers them within days.
- Hiccups, sneezing, cradle cap, baby acne, jaundice in the first week, the umbilical stump falling off.
- He loses some weight after birth and is back to birth weight by about 2 weeks.
- Vaccinations: a cry, a sleepy day, sometimes a fever.
- Teeth start from about 6 months, with drooling and chewing everything.
- Rolling over, sitting up, crawling (some skip it), pulling up on furniture.
- Nappy rash; the first cold from an older sibling; ear infections.

*Daily life*
- Feeds every 2–3 hours at first. Burping, spit-up on everyone's shoulder.
- Day-night confusion in the first weeks.
- First solid food around 6 months: mashed banana, faces at new tastes, food everywhere.
- Bath time, being swaddled, the pram, the car seat, being carried in a sling.
- Contact naps: sleeping only on a person.

*Social*
- Mirroring faces; cooing back and forth like a conversation from about 2 months.
- Social smiling, then laughing at peekaboo and tickles.
- Pointing and following someone's gaze (joint attention) around 9–12 months.
- Waving bye-bye; clapping; recognising his name.
- Being passed around relatives at gatherings.

*Small frustrations*
- A dropped dummy, a wet nappy, being put down, wind, a car seat on a hot day, a sock he cannot pull off.

*Small pleasures*
- Being sung to, a mobile turning above the cot, crinkly toys, his reflection in a mirror, the sound of keys, a
  sibling making silly faces, kicking in the bath, sucking his thumb.

*Firsts*
- First bath, first outing, first holiday, first time sleeping through the night, first tooth, first food, first
  word-like babble ("mama", "dada"), first birthday (which he will not remember).

*Already in:* cuddles, first smile, first laugh, first steps, first word, separation anxiety, teething, antibiotics
and the microbiome, carer attention and phone distraction, attachment.

## 1–3: toddler

**Hormones and body**
- **Adrenaline.** "No" and tantrums are adrenaline running ahead of brain control, before the frontal lobes can
  brake it.
- **Sleep.** He sleeps about 11–14 hours and drops to a single nap.

**Pros:** wonder at everything, rapid language (10–20 new words a week after about 18 months), unshakable
self-belief.
**Cons:** big feelings with no brakes, being told "no" all day, and having no power over anything.

**Joys:** saying "me do it" and doing it, splashing in puddles, the same story twenty times, a new sibling (mixed),
the first friendship at nursery.

**Little joys:** stickers, sand, bubbles, pressing buttons, being chased, a favourite cuddly toy.

**Struggles**
- Potty training, which most children finish between about 2 and 3.
- Biting and hitting at nursery.
- Jealousy of a new sibling.
- Fear of the bath drain or the hoover.
- Bedtime battles.
- Speech delay: about 10–15% of toddlers are "late talkers".

**Sorrows**
- A new sibling takes his parents' attention ("dethroning").
- A nursery key worker he loves leaves.
- His comfort object is lost.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Walking becomes running, climbing and falling; bumps on the forehead and bruised shins.
- Molars coming through around 13–19 months and 2 years.
- Picky eating starts (food neophobia peaks at about 2–6).
- Dropping the bottle; moving from a cot to a bed.
- Learning to use a cup, a spoon, then a fork, with mess.
- First haircut.

*Daily life*
- Routines matter enormously: the same bedtime, the same cup, the same route.
- "Why?" questions begin, and do not stop.
- Copying grown-ups: pretending to cook, talk on the phone, hoover.
- Nursery or a childminder: drop-off tears, pick-up joy, a bag of paintings.
- Playgroups and soft play; the first birthday party with other toddlers.

*Social*
- Parallel play (playing next to, not with, other children) before cooperative play.
- Grabbing toys, "mine!", and learning to take turns.
- Hiding behind a parent's legs when meeting someone new.
- Shows affection: hugs, kisses, bringing toys to show.

*Feelings*
- Pride when he does something himself.
- Fear of loud noises, the dark, being separated.
- Shame begins to appear around 2.
- Comforts himself with a cuddly toy or blanket.

*Small frustrations*
- The wrong colour cup, a broken biscuit, a sock seam, having to leave the park, his shoe on the wrong foot,
  being strapped into the car seat, a sibling touching his things.

*Small pleasures*
- Jumping in puddles, dropping things to watch them fall, pressing every button in a lift, a cardboard box,
  saucepans and a wooden spoon, a ride on a parent's shoulders, feeding ducks, seeing a digger, chasing pigeons.

*Firsts*
- First words joined into sentences, first time saying his own name, first "I love you", first time in a
  swimming pool, first fall that really hurts, first day at nursery.

*Already in:* tantrums, separation anxiety, play, siblings sharing attention.

## 3–6: early childhood

**Hormones and body**
- **Baby teeth.** All 20 are in. He no longer naps, and growth runs steadily at about 5–7 cm a year.
- **Memory.** Lasting memories begin to form; infantile amnesia lifts around 3–4.

**Pros:** imagination and magical thinking, his first real friends, a growing feeling of "I can".
**Cons:** fears (the dark, monsters, dogs), the jolt of starting school, and very little sense of time.

**Joys:** imaginary friends, dressing up, Christmas morning or Eid or Diwali (his family's festivals), the tooth
fairy, his first school friend, learning to swim, the first time he writes his own name.

**Little joys:** a sweet from his grandparent, cartoons, a puddle in wellies, sticker charts, a trip to the park,
staying up late as a treat.

**Struggles**
- Fear of the dark.
- Wetting the bed: about 15% of 5-year-olds.
- Not being able to share.
- Being the "new kid".
- His first experience of being left out.

**Sorrows**
- His first pet dies: the first conversation about death, usually around 4–7.
- His grandparent becomes ill.
- His parents separate, at an age when children often blame themselves.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- He becomes fully toilet-trained, though accidents still happen at night and when he is absorbed in play.
- Hopping, skipping, catching a ball, using scissors, drawing a person (a head with legs at first).
- Left- or right-handedness settles; about 10% are left-handed.
- Childhood illnesses passed around nurseries and schools: chickenpox (where not vaccinated), hand, foot and mouth,
  head lice, threadworms, colds every few weeks.
- Grazed knees, plasters as badges of honour.

*Daily life*
- Starting school: a uniform that is too big, a book bag, the school gate, a packed lunch.
- Phonics and learning letters; counting everything.
- Playdates; swimming lessons; ballet, football or karate for the first time.
- Bedtime stories and the same book every night.
- Screen time battles begin.

*Social*
- Real friendships, but changing week to week ("you're not my friend any more").
- Make-believe games with rules invented on the spot.
- Telling first lies (a normal stage in development, around 3–4).
- Tattling ("Miss, he did...").
- Understanding that others think differently from him (theory of mind, around 4).

*Feelings and inner life*
- Magical thinking: wishes, monsters, Father Christmas, the tooth fairy.
- Big questions: where babies come from, why people die, where the sky ends.
- Fear of getting lost, fear of being told off, fear of the doctor.
- Jealousy, sulking, and making up quickly.

*Small frustrations*
- Losing at a game, being told it is bedtime, not being allowed the big slide, a sibling copying him, a broken
  crayon, the wrong cereal, having his hair washed.

*Small pleasures*
- Collecting stones and leaves, a new pair of shoes that light up, painting at school, a party bag, the swings,
  a story read in funny voices, his drawing on the fridge, a sticker for being brave at the doctor.

*Firsts*
- First day of school, first time sleeping at a grandparent's, first time riding a scooter, first swimming badge,
  first nativity play or school performance, first time writing his name.

*Already in:* nightmares, losing his first tooth, lost toy, best friend, starting school, festival and holiday joy as
a generic snow day.

## 6–11: middle childhood

**Hormones and body**
- **Adrenarche (6–8).** DHEA rises from the adrenal glands, bringing body odour, early body hair, more oily skin and
  sometimes moodiness. It is the quiet start of puberty.
- **Early puberty in girls.** In the US, the average age breasts begin to develop is now about 9–10, and earlier
  still with obesity and endocrine disruptors.
- **Myopia.** Short-sightedness is rising with screen and indoor time; a large share of children in East Asia now
  need glasses.

**Pros:** competence (reading, riding, building), best friends, long summers, low stress for many children.
**Cons:** the first comparing ("she's cleverer than me"), the pecking order in the playground, the first exam
pressure in some regions.

**Joys:** sleepovers, a first collection (cards, stickers, shells), learning a game's rules and winning, the school
trip, Scouts or a team, building a den, his first chapter book, a first proper hobby (chess, coding, football).

**Little joys:** the ice-cream van, the last day of term, a snow day, a new pencil case, finding a good stick, his
favourite TV show, his name read out in assembly.

**Struggles**
- A learning difficulty showing up: dyslexia affects about 5–10% of children.
- Glasses or braces, and being teased for them.
- Being picked last.
- Tests at school.
- Times-tables anxiety.
- Fear of a parent dying.
- Climate anxiety reaching children: more than half of young people say they are very worried (Hickman 2021).

**Sorrows**
- A grandparent dies, often his first death of a person.
- His best friend moves away.
- His parents' divorce and two homes: about 1 in 3 children in the US experience their parents splitting up.
- His pet dies.
- He is bullied.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Adult teeth come in looking too big; wobbly teeth; the gap-toothed school photo.
- Growing out of shoes every few months.
- Eye tests and the first pair of glasses; orthodontist visits.
- Headlice outbreaks; verrucas from the swimming pool.
- Stamina for long walks, bike rides, swimming lengths.
- Some children start to put on weight; childhood obesity is common in many countries.

*Daily life*
- Homework, reading logs, spelling tests, times tables.
- School trips, sports day, harvest festivals, assemblies.
- After-school clubs; music lessons and practice he does not want to do.
- Pocket money: saving up for something for weeks.
- First chores: making his bed, laying the table.
- Walking to school alone for the first time, later in many countries than it used to be.

*Social*
- Best friends and enemies; secret clubs and dens.
- Playground games and crazes (cards, slime, fidget toys, a new video game).
- Birthday party politics: who invites whom.
- Teasing about names, clothes, looks.
- Gender divides in play are strongest at this age.
- Grown-ups' rules begin to look unfair.

*Feelings and inner life*
- A strong sense of fairness.
- Hero worship of an older child, a footballer or a YouTuber.
- Worries about tests, about parents arguing, about the news.
- Embarrassment about his parents begins.
- Collecting facts obsessively (dinosaurs, planets, footballers).

*Small frustrations*
- Losing a game at the last minute, a pen running out, homework on a sunny day, being made to wear a coat, his
  sibling getting something he did not, having to stop a game for dinner.

*Small pleasures*
- A completed sticker album, the smell of a new book, a win on a video game, being chosen for a part in the play,
  a fire drill, a supply teacher, a pancake day, building with Lego for hours, a sleepover with midnight snacks.

*Firsts*
- First time staying up until midnight on New Year's Eve, first mobile phone in some families, first time going to
  the shop alone, first school residential trip, first time beating a parent at a game.

*Already in:* reading, riding a bike, being picked last, not invited to a party, sleepovers, parents fighting, prizes,
bullying, clubs, school belonging, a first phone, cyberbullying.

## 11–14: early adolescence

**Hormones and body**
- **Puberty.** GnRH switches on, and testosterone rises up to about 20 times in boys, while estrogen and
  progesterone rise in girls. The growth spurt can add about 10 cm a year at its peak.
- **Body changes.** Girls start their periods at an average age of about 12. Boys' voices break, they have their
  first wet dreams, and they grow their first facial hair.
- **Sleep clock.** Melatonin is released about 2 hours later than before, so teenagers are biologically night owls
  while school starts early. The result is chronic sleep debt.
- **Reward and stress.** The brain's reward system peaks in sensitivity, especially to rewards seen by friends: risk
  taking is greater with an audience. Cortisol reactions to social judgement rise sharply.
- **Skin.** Acne affects about 85% of adolescents.

**Pros:** a sense of independence, deep friendships, discovering music and identity, and being able to think about
abstract ideas.
**Cons:** self-consciousness peaks (the "imaginary audience": feeling watched by everyone), the body changes in public,
and parents suddenly feel embarrassing.

**Joys:** a first crush, first trips into town without parents, the first time he chooses his own clothes and
music, a group chat that feels like a home, gaming all night with friends.

**Little joys:** a song that feels written for him, a growth spurt that finally makes him tall, a teacher who gets
him, the first time he goes out without saying where.

**Struggles**
- Changing for PE.
- Starting his periods at school (girls).
- Early puberty in girls and late puberty in boys both carry social costs.
- The move up to secondary school.
- Social media comparison sets in.
- Sleep deprivation.
- His first real exams.

**Sorrows**
- His friendship group splits up.
- He realises his parents are fallible.
- He loses his childhood interests ("too old for that"), and with them part of who he was.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Growth spurts bring clumsiness: limbs grow before coordination catches up.
- Growing pains in the legs at night.
- Body odour; the first deodorant; sweaty PE kit.
- Girls: breast development, the first bra, period products, period pain.
- Boys: voice cracks, body hair, the first shave, unexpected erections, sometimes breast tissue swelling
  (gynaecomastia), which usually passes.
- Appetite becomes enormous.
- He needs about 9 hours of sleep but gets fewer.

*Daily life*
- A bigger school: moving between classrooms, many teachers, timetables, lockers.
- Getting lost in the new school; older pupils seem huge.
- More homework; detentions.
- Taking the bus alone; going into town with friends.
- The phone becomes the centre of social life.

*Social*
- Friendship groups form and break; cliques; "popular" kids.
- Crushes talked about endlessly; "going out" with someone without really meeting.
- Fear of being laughed at; obsessing about what to wear.
- Parents' rules feel humiliating; arguments about screen time and bedtime.
- Group chats that include and exclude.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Mood swings; feeling everything intensely.
- Daydreaming; keeping a diary.
- Taking music, fandoms, games or a sport very seriously.
- First real awareness of the wider world: politics, injustice, climate.
- Privacy matters suddenly; the bedroom door is closed.

*Small frustrations*
- A spot before the school photo, the wrong trainers, a parent shouting his name at the school gate, the Wi-Fi
  going down, a teacher reading out his mark, being treated like a child.

*Small pleasures*
- A new song on repeat, a funny video sent at midnight, a cancelled lesson, a friend saving him a seat, the
  first time his parent lets him stay home alone, getting taller than a parent.

*Firsts*
- First time walking home in the dark, first time going to the cinema without an adult, first social media
  account, first slow dance at a school disco, first time he swears in front of a parent.

*Already in:* first crush, voice breaking, first period, mirror and body image, puberty and how it outruns the
frontal lobes, crisis news, echo chambers, phone habits.

## 14–18: late adolescence

**Hormones and body**
- **Adult levels.** Testosterone reaches near-adult levels and estrogen cycles settle. Boys keep growing until about
  17–18.
- **Sexuality.** The first sexual experience comes at a median age of about 17 in the US.
- **Brain.** The prefrontal cortex is still wiring until about 25, and emotional regulation lags behind feeling.

**Pros:** freedom (a driving licence, a part-time job, nights out), intense friendships, first love, the first
taste of adult choices.
**Cons:** exam pressure, decisions about his future made too early, pressure to drink or vape, and peak
risk-taking.

**Joys:** first love, a first proper relationship, festivals, a road trip with friends, an exam result that opens a
door, his school prom, getting into the university he wanted.

**Little joys:** late-night food runs, 3 am conversations with friends, a playlist for every mood, a lie-in on a
Sunday, the first time someone calls him a man or a woman.

**Struggles**
- Choosing a university or a path.
- Vaping: about 1 in 10 US high school students vape.
- His first hangover, and drink-driving risk.
- His first sexual experience and the anxiety around it; sexual health; teen pregnancy (with large regional
  differences).
- Coming out.
- Self-harm, which peaks in adolescence, especially in girls.
- Fallouts in group chats.

**Sorrows**
- His first heartbreak.
- He realises his family is not perfect.
- A friend dies young (rare, but it shakes a whole school).
- Leaving his childhood home and friends.
- Being rejected by the university or college he wanted.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Peak height reached; some boys keep growing into their late teens.
- Muscle comes with training; sports peak for many.
- Acne treatments, skincare routines, hair dye, piercings, tattoos (where allowed).
- Teeth braces come off.
- Sleep: late nights, early alarms, lying in until noon at weekends.
- First experiences of alcohol, cigarettes, vapes or cannabis, depending on friends and place.

*Daily life*
- Revision timetables, mock exams, results day.
- Part-time jobs: waiting tables, a shop counter, babysitting, deliveries.
- Learning to drive: lessons, stalling at junctions, the theory test.
- University or job applications; personal statements; open days; interviews.
- Getting a first bank card; running out of money before payday.

*Social*
- First relationships: texting all night, meeting the parents, anniversaries counted in months.
- Parties: someone's house when the parents are away; walking home at 3 am.
- Fake IDs and getting into clubs underage, in some places.
- Sneaking out; lying about where he was.
- Friendships that feel like they will last forever, and sometimes do.
- Pressure to look a certain way and to have had certain experiences by a certain age.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Wanting to be taken seriously.
- Big ideals: fighting injustice, changing the world.
- Questioning religion and his parents' beliefs.
- Fear of the future; fear of failure.
- Nostalgia for childhood starting already.

*Small frustrations*
- A curfew, being asked "what are your plans?", a sibling borrowing clothes, the bus not coming, his phone on 1%,
  a group project where no one else works.

*Small pleasures*
- Driving with the windows down, a summer job paycheck, staying out late with permission, inside jokes with best
  friends, last day of exams, a crush replying, wearing something he bought himself.

*Firsts*
- First sexual experience, first job interview, first hangover, first time voting (in some places at 16–18), first
  passport photo as an adult, first time he drives alone, the last day of school.

*Already in:* first kiss, heartbreak, concerts, driving test, first wage, getting drunk for the first time, feeling
misunderstood, wondering "who am I", peer pressure, inside jokes, big exams, choosing subjects, coming out,
eating disorders and anxiety, prejudice.

## 18–25: emerging adulthood

**Hormones and body**
- **Peak body.** Physical peak: testosterone peaks in the late teens and twenties, VO2 max and reaction times peak,
  and bone mass keeps building until about 30. Hangovers are mild (they worsen with age).
- **Brain.** The prefrontal cortex finishes maturing around 25.
- **Mental health.** Psychosis and bipolar disorder most often first appear in these years.

**Pros:** freedom, energy, recovering quickly, the widest friend networks (friend numbers peak at about 25), big
reinventions, low commitments.
**Cons:** instability (jobs, housing, relationships), low pay, the comparison trap, and feeling lost without the
structure of school.

**Joys:** moving out, his first own room, travelling on a shoestring, university life, his first salary, a
relationship that lasts, a first big achievement at work, finding his people.

**Little joys:** cheap meals with flatmates, cooking his first decent meal, a house party, a long lie-in, spending
his own money on something frivolous, a new city at night.

**Struggles**
- Homesickness in the first term: about 1 in 3 students.
- Grade and career anxiety.
- Loneliness: people aged 16–29 report loneliness most often in the UK (ONS).
- Unpaid internships, rent, entry-level jobs being automated, debt.
- Dating apps.
- Friendships that end when university ends.
- His first serious illness or injury.

**Sorrows**
- The end of his first long relationship.
- Losing touch with school friends.
- A parent's first serious illness.
- His dream not working out.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Recovering from late nights in a day; later, needing two.
- Cooking for himself for the first time: pasta, toast, takeaways, weight gained in the first year.
- Gym habits or none; the first running app.
- Sexual health check-ups; contraception decisions.
- Wisdom teeth coming through or removed.
- Getting his own doctor and dentist for the first time.

*Daily life*
- Shared flats: the washing-up rota, a flatmate's boyfriend who never leaves, a fridge with labelled food.
- Freshers' week; lectures he skips; deadlines met at 4 am.
- Managing money alone for the first time; overdrafts; bills he did not know existed.
- Laundry: the first shrunk jumper and pink shirts.
- Night buses, cheap flights, sleeping on friends' floors.
- First full-time job: commuting, a lanyard, office politics, Monday dread, Friday drinks.
- Doing his own tax return or forms for the first time.

*Social*
- New friends everywhere; then the circle narrows to the ones who matter.
- Long-distance relationships with school sweethearts that often end by the first Christmas.
- Dating apps, situationships, first "real" adult relationships.
- Friends' first engagements and the first wedding invitations.
- Visiting home: being treated like a teenager again in his old room.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Freedom mixed with feeling lost.
- Identity experiments: style, beliefs, politics, faith, sexuality.
- Realising adults are making it up as they go along.
- Nostalgia for school and home.
- The first "I'm an adult now" moments, often around small things like buying a kettle.

*Small frustrations*
- Landlords who ignore repairs, mould in the bathroom, a deposit not returned, a flatmate who never cleans,
  an alarm clock after a late night, being asked "so what are you going to do with that degree?"

*Small pleasures*
- A full fridge he paid for, a Sunday with nothing to do, a cheap holiday with friends, a big night out, a late
  breakfast, his first plant, a parcel arriving for him, a parent's food when visiting home.

*Firsts*
- First time renting, first time signing a contract, first salary, first credit card, first time paying for a
  meal for his parents, first funeral as an adult, first time living with a partner.

*Already in:* first pay cheque, travelling alone, feeling behind, eating alone in a new city, finding his people,
being ghosted, dating apps, a dream job, money worries, starting university, dropping out, job hunting, moving out,
the quarter-life feeling.

## 25–35: early adulthood

**Hormones and body**
- **Fertility.** Female fertility starts falling from about 32 and faster after 37. Sperm quality falls slowly from
  about 35–40.
- **Metabolism and bone.** Metabolism stays mostly stable (Pontzer 2021: energy use holds from 20 to 60). Bone mass
  peaks around 30.
- **Testosterone.** It starts declining by about 1% a year after 30–40.
- **Pregnancy (for him as a mother, or his partner).** hCG, progesterone, a 50-fold estrogen surge, morning sickness
  in about 70%, then a hormonal crash after birth and the "baby blues" in up to 80% of mothers.
- **Fatherhood.** Fathers also gain weight in pregnancy (sympathetic weight), and some show couvade syndrome
  (sympathetic symptoms).
- **Sleep.** A new parent loses about 400–700 hours of sleep in the first year.

**Pros:** competence, a career forming, chosen family, a partnership, his own home, children.
**Cons:** everything at once: career, partner, children, a mortgage. Friends get busier, and the first signs of
ageing appear.

**Joys:** his wedding, a pregnancy announcement, the baby's first laugh, first holidays as a family, a promotion,
buying a home, a dog of his own.

**Little joys:** coffee before the house wakes up, a weekend with nothing planned, a friend's wedding, a good
haircut, a nap with the baby, his first good piece of furniture.

**Struggles**
- His own friendships fade. Making new friends takes about 50 hours together for a casual friend and about 200
  for a close one (Hall 2018).
- The workload in the household is split unevenly.
- The couple's relationship satisfaction dips after children.
- The cost of childcare.
- Juggling work and family.
- His own health: back pain, weight gain, his first blood-pressure reading.
- Pressure to have children from family and culture.
- The biological clock.

**Sorrows**
- Miscarriage.
- Infertility.
- Divorce: the median first marriage that ends in divorce lasts about 8 years.
- A parent's illness.
- Friends moving away.
- Dreams set aside for stability.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Hangovers start lasting two days.
- He notices his first grey hairs, a receding hairline in some men, and his first wrinkles around the eyes.
- Back twinges from sitting all day at a desk.
- He learns which foods disagree with him; lactose intolerance and reflux are common.
- Running a first 5k or marathon, or joining a gym in January and stopping in February.
- Pregnancy for a partner (or himself): scans, cravings, a growing bump, a hospital bag packed by the door.

*Daily life*
- Commutes, calendars and meetings.
- Weddings: stag and hen weekends, speeches, a suit he wears once a year.
- Moving in together: whose sofa stays, how to load the dishwasher.
- Mortgage applications, surveys, solicitors, moving day.
- New parenthood: nappies at 3 am, the pram that will not fold, baby groups, nursery waiting lists, a car seat
  fitted in the car park.
- Weekends shift from nights out to errands, DIY and visiting family.

*Social*
- Friend meet-ups need planning weeks ahead.
- Group chats turn into baby photos.
- Couple friends; dinner parties instead of clubs.
- Visiting in-laws; holidays split between families.
- Colleagues become friends, or the only people he sees.

*Feelings and inner life*
- The clock: "where am I supposed to be by 30?"
- Settling: deciding what he really values.
- Guilt about not seeing his parents enough.
- Fear of making the wrong big choice: job, partner, place to live.
- Contentment in routines he once thought were boring.

*Small frustrations*
- A wedding season that costs a month's salary, a train cancellation, the car's MOT, a baby waking just as he
  sits down, an email at 6 pm on a Friday, being asked "when are you having kids?"

*Small pleasures*
- A good night's sleep, a new sofa, a plant that survives, his own front door, a takeaway on a Friday, the baby
  falling asleep on his chest, a long bath, a dinner where everyone stays late.

*Firsts*
- First home, first child, first pet as an adult, first time being someone's boss, first time he feels old at a
  festival, first time paying for a funeral or a wedding.

*Already in:* wedding, buying a home, impostor syndrome, going to weddings single, friends drifting, pregnancy,
miscarriage, stillbirth, infertility and IVF, father's hormones and postnatal depression, newborn, promotion,
burnout, long hours, partner.

## 35–45: the thick of it

**Hormones and body**
- **Perimenopause.** It can start in the early 40s and last 4–8 years, with irregular cycles, sleep problems, mood
  swings, anxiety, brain fog and heavier periods.
- **Men.** Testosterone keeps falling slowly and SHBG rises, so free testosterone drops further. Belly fat builds up.
- **Muscle and eyes.** Muscle loss of about 3–8% per decade begins. Close-up vision fades (presbyopia, from about 40).
- **Cortisol.** Chronic work and family load flattens the daily cortisol rhythm.

**Pros:** peak earning and expertise, settled identity, children becoming people, a clearer sense of what
matters.
**Cons:** time poverty, the "sandwich" of caring for children and parents at once, a career plateau, the body
starting to send bills.

**Joys:** his child's first school play, his child's first goal, a career-defining project, coaching his child's
team, renovating a home, his first big adventure holiday as a family.

**Little joys:** a quiet car journey alone, the kids asleep, wine with friends, a good night's sleep, his child
saying "I love you" unprompted.

**Struggles**
- Burnout peaks.
- Caring for a parent while raising children.
- Fights with teenage children.
- Disagreements between partners about parenting.
- Mortgage stress.
- A health scare.
- Weight gain.
- Friends' divorces.
- Affairs: about 20% of married men and 13% of married women in the US say they have had one.

**Sorrows**
- His parents begin to fail.
- A friend dies of cancer or a heart attack.
- He mourns the paths he didn't take, the start of regret over inaction.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Metabolism changes less than he thinks, but activity falls, so weight creeps up.
- Recovery from injury and exercise slows.
- He needs reading glasses for small print, holding menus further away.
- Snoring, waking at 4 am with thoughts racing.
- Aches after sleeping in a strange bed.
- Health checks: blood pressure, cholesterol, his first mole check.

*Daily life*
- The school run, packed lunches, lost PE kits, parents' evenings.
- Taxi service for his children's clubs and parties.
- Work responsibility: managing people, budgets and deadlines.
- Holidays planned around school terms and costs.
- Parents' doctor appointments slotted between meetings.
- Paperwork: wills, life insurance, pensions, school applications.

*Social*
- Friends are mostly other parents.
- Old friends seen once or twice a year.
- Marriages around him strain; some friends divorce.
- His teenagers want less of him; his parents want more.

*Feelings and inner life*
- "Where did the time go?"
- Pride in his children, envy of others' careers, worry about money.
- Guilt: not enough time for work, children, partner, parents or himself.
- Wondering about the road not taken.
- Moments of deep contentment in an ordinary evening at home.

*Small frustrations*
- A school WhatsApp group with 200 messages, a parking ticket at the hospital, lost keys, homework he cannot
  help with, a boiler breaking in winter, a teenager rolling their eyes.

*Small pleasures*
- A quiet coffee in the car before going in, his child's drawing on the office wall, a family film night, a
  weekend away without the kids, a well-fitting pair of jeans, a compliment at work.

*Firsts*
- First time his child is taller than him, first time he feels like the adult at a family gathering, first serious
  conversation with a parent about their wishes when they die.

*Already in:* asking "is this it?", body changes, a health scare, the sandwich years, caring for a parent, being
passed over, an old friend, a new hobby, friends splitting up, midlife low, regrets.

## 45–55: midlife

**Hormones and body**
- **Menopause.** The average age is 51. Estrogen falls sharply: hot flushes in about 75% of women, fast bone loss,
  vaginal dryness, poorer sleep, and higher heart risk afterwards. Some women feel freer and calmer after it.
- **"Andropause".** Gradual in men: lower energy, libido and muscle, and more fat around the middle. Low
  testosterone is linked to low mood.
- **Diet and eyes.** Insulin sensitivity falls; type 2 diabetes risk rises. He needs reading glasses.
- **Other.** Thinning hair, greying, joints, snoring, and the first colonoscopy.

**Pros:** wisdom, confidence, financial stability for many, children leaving and freedom returning, deep long
friendships.
**Cons:** the lowest point of the wellbeing curve (about 47–48), ageing parents, fear of becoming irrelevant at work,
the empty nest.

**Joys:** his child's graduation, paying off the mortgage, rediscovering his partner, his first grandchild (for
some), travelling as a couple, mastering a craft.

**Little joys:** a lie-in with no school run, gardening, a long walk, cooking a slow meal, a good book on holiday,
hearing from his grown child just to chat.

**Struggles**
- Age discrimination at work starts.
- Redundancy.
- A parent with dementia.
- Hot flushes in meetings (women).
- Erectile dysfunction: about 40% of men at 40 and about 50% at 50 have some degree of it.
- Rising cholesterol and blood pressure.
- Grey divorce: divorce over 50 has doubled since 1990 in the US.

**Sorrows**
- A parent dies, often for the first time in these years.
- He becomes the "oldest generation".
- His children leave.
- A friend dies.
- A lump or a scan.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Hot flushes and night sweats for women; fans at the bedside.
- Joint aches in the morning; knees on stairs.
- Hearing in noisy restaurants starts to fade.
- Sleep becomes lighter; he wakes at night to use the toilet.
- Hair thinning and greying; eyebrows and ear hair growing.
- The first colonoscopy, mammogram or prostate check.
- Weight around the middle; trying a diet for the first time.

*Daily life*
- Teenagers learning to drive: white knuckles in the passenger seat.
- Children leaving home: university drop-offs, an empty bedroom, a quieter fridge.
- Caring for parents: hospital visits, sorting their paperwork, clearing their home.
- Work: senior, respected, or quietly sidelined.
- Paying off the mortgage, or worrying it will never be paid off.

*Social*
- Reconnecting with old friends on social media; school reunions.
- Friends' illnesses: the first friend with cancer.
- Couples rediscover each other, or realise they have little left in common.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Midlife reassessment: what to keep, what to drop.
- A new hobby or passion: cycling, running, a band, a language.
- Feeling invisible (often reported by women), or freer for it.
- Time becoming precious.

*Small frustrations*
- Forgetting why he came into a room, needing glasses to read a label, a child who never calls, a parent who
  repeats the same story, technology updates.

*Small pleasures*
- The house to himself, a whole Sunday reading, a child phoning just to chat, a garden that finally looks good,
  sleeping in, a holiday without children for the first time in years.

*Firsts*
- First time he outlives a friend, first time being called "sir" or "madam" by someone young, first time he cries
  at his child's graduation, first time he becomes a grandparent (for some).

*Already in:* menopause (for him if female), empty nest, rediscovering his partner, his child thanking him, a parent's
death, dementia, skill obsolescence, the midlife dip.

---

## 55–65: late working years

**Hormones and body**
- **Post-menopause.** Bone density keeps falling (osteoporosis in about 1 in 3 women over 50 worldwide) and heart
  risk rises.
- **Men.** Prostate enlargement and getting up at night to urinate (nocturia).
- **Sleep.** Melatonin falls and his body clock shifts earlier (early to bed, early awake), and deep sleep shrinks.
- **Heart and senses.** Blood pressure rises; about 1 in 3 have hypertension. Hearing starts to fade. Muscle loss
  speeds up.

**Pros:** grandparenthood, the end of work in sight, freedom, settled finances (for some), wisdom, choosing
how to spend his time.
**Cons:** the body starts limiting him, friends get ill, ageism at work, the fear that his savings won't last.

**Joys:** a grandchild, retirement planning and dreams, a big trip abroad, a milestone birthday with everyone,
walking his child down the aisle.

**Little joys:** a morning walk, crosswords, the garden in spring, babysitting a grandchild, a new recipe.

**Struggles**
- Chronic illness: about 6 in 10 US adults have at least one chronic condition, and more at these ages.
- Joint replacement.
- Caring for a very old parent.
- An adult child in difficulty: returning home, or addiction.

**Sorrows**
- The death of the last parent ("orphaned" at 60).
- Losing a peer.
- His identity shifts as work ends.
- His partner becomes ill.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Stiffness after sitting; getting up off the floor with a hand on the furniture.
- Skin thinner and drier; bruises easier.
- Memory: names slip more, which worries him more than it should.
- Medicines start: statins, blood pressure pills, a pill organiser.
- Taste and smell begin to fade a little.

*Daily life*
- Counting down to retirement; pension statements read carefully for the first time.
- Helping with grandchildren: school pick-ups, holiday childcare.
- Sorting out a parent's estate; selling the family home.
- Downsizing conversations.
- More time in the garden, on walks, on the allotment.

*Social*
- Retirement parties for colleagues.
- Friends move away to the coast or to be near grandchildren.
- Funerals become more frequent than weddings.
- Clubs, choirs, walking groups, volunteering.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Gratitude for what has gone right.
- Anxiety about health, money and loneliness ahead.
- Thinking about legacy.
- Rediscovering old interests from youth.

*Small frustrations*
- Passwords, app updates, small print, hold music on the phone, a bad knee on a walk, a grandchild correcting his
  technology.

*Small pleasures*
- The first morning without an alarm after retiring, a midweek trip when everywhere is quiet, a grandchild's hand
  in his, a long lunch with an old friend, a newspaper crossword finished.

*Firsts*
- First day of retirement, first senior discount, first time a stranger offers him a seat, first time he becomes
  the oldest in the family.

*Already in:* retirement and its identity shift, grandchildren, pension worries, scams, insulin resistance, his
savings running out, more years behind him than ahead, peace.

---

## 65–75: young-old

**Hormones and body**
- **Hormones.** DHEA is about 20% of its peak, and melatonin is low. Vasopressin's night-time rise weakens, so he
  wakes at night to urinate.
- **Thirst and appetite.** He feels less thirsty and so drinks less. Food tastes and smells fainter.
- **Senses.** Cataracts affect about half of people by 75. Hearing loss affects about a third of over-65s.
- **Dopamine.** Receptors fall by about 10% a decade, so novelty lifts him less, but contentment often rises.

**Pros:** time, grandchildren, freedom from proving himself, positive memories favoured over negative ones (the
"positivity effect"), and often the happiest years of later life.
**Cons:** illness becomes routine, friends begin to die, technology moves on without him, and he loses status.

**Joys:** volunteering, travel, learning something new (a language, painting, the university of the third age),
grandchildren sleeping over, a long marriage.

**Little joys:** a weekday matinee, a bird at the feeder, an unhurried breakfast, a phone call from a grandchild,
a nap in an armchair.

**Struggles**
- Hearing aids.
- Several medicines at once: about 40% take 5 or more.
- Losing a partner, and with it his role as a couple.
- Isolation.
- Retirement boredom.
- Falls begin.
- Scams target him.
- Cancer rates peak.

**Sorrows**
- Widowhood: about a third of women over 65 are widows.
- A sibling dies.
- He gives up his home or his car.
- A friend's dementia.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Hearing aids, varifocals, dentures or implants.
- Walking slows; a stick on long walks for some.
- Afternoon naps; early nights, early mornings.
- Feeling the cold more.
- Constipation and digestion becoming a topic of conversation.
- Vaccines: flu, shingles, pneumonia.

*Daily life*
- A routine that gives shape to the days: the paper, a walk, a programme, the shops.
- Grandchildren's activities and sports days.
- Hospital appointments: waiting rooms and car parks.
- Learning to use video calls to see family abroad.
- Holidays: cruises, coach tours, trips to see children who moved away.

*Social*
- Friends become precious; fewer and closer.
- Widowhood among friends; friends' second marriages.
- Volunteering, church, the bowls club, the community centre.
- Being the person younger relatives ask about family history.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Contentment and calm, often more than in midlife.
- Fear of dementia; noticing every forgotten word.
- Worry about being a burden.
- Pride in grandchildren.
- Thinking more about death, often with less fear than when younger.

*Small frustrations*
- Websites he cannot navigate, self-checkout tills, steps without a rail, a grandchild who does not call,
  small print on medicine, buttons on a remote.

*Small pleasures*
- Birdsong in the morning, a good crossword, a cup of tea in the garden, a letter in the post, a visit from a
  grandchild, a favourite old film, the first warm day of spring.

*Firsts*
- First hearing aid, first great-grandchild for some, first time he uses a walking aid, first time he moves his
  bed downstairs.

*Already in:* hearing aids, peace, telling stories to his grandchildren, feeling left behind by technology, being
scammed, downsizing, widowhood and its grief.

## 75–85: old-old

**Hormones and body**
- **Muscle and bone.** Sarcopenia (muscle loss) and frailty, and a hip fracture risk that rises steeply.
- **Brain.** Dementia affects about 1 in 10 people over 65 and about 1 in 3 over 85.
- **Sleep.** His sleep is fragmented, and he naps more.
- **Body.** He loses height, his thermoregulation weakens (heat waves are deadly), and his immune system weakens
  (pneumonia, shingles).

**Pros:** wisdom, letting go of what does not matter, deep gratitude, being cherished as the head of the family.
**Cons:** loss after loss, dependency, outliving his friends, fear of becoming a burden.

**Joys:** great-grandchildren, a family reunion, his birthday with the family, a visit from an old friend, a
garden that still grows.

**Little joys:** sun on his face, a favourite song from his youth (musical memory survives dementia longest), a hand
held, a familiar radio show, a cup of tea made by someone else.

**Struggles**
- Falls.
- Moving into care.
- Incontinence.
- Losing his independence.
- Loneliness.
- Being talked down to (elderspeak).
- Sight loss from macular degeneration.
- Constipation and digestion.
- Physical and financial abuse of older people affects about 1 in 6 people over 60 (WHO).

**Sorrows**
- His friends die one after another.
- His partner dies.
- He loses his home.
- He loses his memory and, with it, himself.
- His adult child dies before him.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Balance becomes a daily concern: rugs, stairs, wet floors.
- Fatigue after small tasks; days planned around energy.
- Appetite smaller; food tasting bland.
- Skin tears easily; slower healing.
- Sleep in short pieces through the day and night.

*Daily life*
- Carers or family helping with shopping, cleaning, washing.
- Grab rails, a stairlift, a walking frame, an alarm pendant.
- Meals on wheels or ready meals.
- Pills in a weekly box; pharmacy deliveries.
- The television on for company.

*Social*
- Christmas cards with fewer names each year.
- Being visited rather than visiting.
- Day centres, lunch clubs.
- Friendship with carers and neighbours.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Living in memories; telling the same stories.
- Grief layered on grief.
- Fear of hospital, of care homes, of being alone at night.
- Acceptance and calm for many; anger and frustration for some.

*Small frustrations*
- Not being able to open a jar, buttons and zips, dropping things, people speaking too fast, being rushed,
  forms to fill in.

*Small pleasures*
- A grandchild's phone call, an old photo album, sunshine through a window, a familiar song, a biscuit with tea,
  a chat with the postman, a pet on his lap.

*Firsts and lasts*
- Last time driving, last holiday, last time living in his own home for some, first night in a care home.

*Already in:* giving up the car, falls, forgetting names, a silent week, dementia (as neurodegeneration), loss of
third spaces, digital-only services.

## 85+: oldest-old

**Hormones and body**
- **Frailty.** About a quarter of people over 85 are frail. They eat less (the "anorexia of ageing") and dehydrate
  easily.
- **Senses and swallowing.** Swallowing becomes harder. Hearing and sight are often both impaired.
- **Near the end.** A slow "terminal decline" in wellbeing in the last 3–5 years of life (Gerstorf).

**Pros:** what Tornstam called gerotranscendence: a more cosmic and less self-centred outlook, peace with death,
being a living link to history.
**Cons:** almost total dependence, chronic pain, being the last one left.

**Joys:** being visited, being asked about the past, holding a great-grandchild, a good day.

**Little joys:** being read to, a warm drink, a window with a view, a pet visit, being touched gently.

**Struggles:** pain, sleep, being in care, losing control over daily life.

**Sorrows**
- Outliving everyone, including sometimes his own children.
- Preparing for death.
- Saying goodbye.

**Minor details**

*Body and senses*
- Hearing and sight both limited; conversations harder.
- Swallowing difficulties; thickened drinks.
- Pressure sores if bed-bound.
- Very low energy; long sleeps.

*Daily life*
- Days measured in meals, visits and naps.
- Care routines: washing, dressing, moving.
- Hospital stays after falls or infections.

*Social*
- Visits from family; great-grandchildren brought in.
- Being the last of his generation; no one left who knew him young.

*Feelings and inner life*
- Peace for many; confusion for some.
- Reliving the past; sometimes believing it is the past.
- Wanting to go home, even when he is home.
- Saying what he needs to say to the people he loves.

*Small pleasures*
- A hand held, a familiar voice, music from his youth, the smell of his favourite food, a warm blanket, a window
  with a view of the sky.

*Lasts*
- Last birthday, last conversation, last goodbyes.

*Already in:* legacy (writing down what he wants his family to know), regrets in his life review, peace.

## Across every age (not yet in)

**Hormones and body**
- **Menstrual cycle (if female).** Monthly mood and energy shifts, PMS in about 30%, PMDD in about 5%, and period
  pain.
- **Daily rhythms.** Cortisol peaks on waking and serotonin rises with sunlight. Seasonal affective disorder affects
  about 5% in northern countries; the daylight cycle exists in the simulation, but SAD does not.
- **Sex and love.** Being in love brings a dopamine and norepinephrine high for the first 1–2 years, then
  oxytocin-based attachment. A good sex life lifts couples' wellbeing.
- **Illness and injury.** Chronic pain affects about 1 in 5 adults. Concussion; long COVID; allergies; migraines
  (about 15% of people); tinnitus.

**Joys:** religion and spiritual experience, awe (night skies, mountains), creating something (art, music, writing),
sport and team wins, the national team winning, a good meal with people he loves, laughter, being forgiven.

**Struggles**
- Addiction beyond alcohol: gambling, nicotine, cannabis, opioids.
- Debt crises.
- Crime: being a victim, and being a perpetrator (with jail).
- War and displacement, and migration.
- Natural disasters (some already exist).
- Poverty traps.
- Legal trouble.
- Disability acquired through life, and accidents (road deaths peak at 15–29).
- Pandemics.

**Sorrows**
- Suicide loss: a friend or relative who dies by suicide. This grief is especially hard and stigmatised.
- Ambiguous loss: someone missing, or a family member lost to addiction.
- Grieving a pet as a family member.
- Losing his faith.
- Losing his homeland.

**Minor details at any age**

*Weather and seasons*
- The first warm day, the first snow, a storm at night, a heat wave that keeps him awake, long summer evenings,
  dark winter afternoons.

*Home*
- A broken boiler, a leaking roof, a noisy neighbour, a new kitchen, a house that finally feels like home.

*Money*
- An unexpected bill, a tax refund, a pay rise, a sale on something he wanted, losing his wallet, a small win on
  a scratch card.

*Body*
- A cold, a stubbed toe, a paper cut, a good haircut, a bad haircut, new glasses, a sore tooth.

*Everyday luck*
- Finding money in an old coat, catching a train just in time, a parking space right outside, a delayed flight,
  the queue moving fast, rain on a planned day out.

*Connection*
- A message from someone he was thinking about, a compliment from a stranger, a hug at the right moment, an
  argument that ends well, being remembered on his birthday, being forgotten on it.

*Food*
- A favourite meal, a new restaurant, a burnt dinner, the smell of baking, a picnic, food that reminds him of
  childhood.

*Culture*
- A film that changes how he thinks, a book he cannot put down, a song that brings back a memory, a game that
  takes over a weekend, a TV series finale.
